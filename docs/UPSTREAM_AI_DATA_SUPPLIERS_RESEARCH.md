# The suppliers behind Claude, ChatGPT and frontier AI

**Project:** Super Sovereign Source LLM IP  
**Research date:** October 10, 2026  
**Scope:** Training data, expert work, licensed content, evaluations, learning environments and the communities around their production. Public-source research; no private contracts or private forum discussions inspected.

The most useful upstream companies to investigate first are **Surge AI, Turing, Mercor and Invisible Technologies** for expert-created training data, and **Created by Humans and Defined.ai** for content licensing. Their evidence differs: some publish named lab relationships, others have reported customers, and others are prospective suppliers without a verified OpenAI or Anthropic buyer. The tables below preserve those distinctions.

For Sovereign Source, the opportunity extends beyond buying datasets. A contributor syndicate could itself supply licensed works, expert demonstrations, critiques, evaluation tasks and documented professional workflows. Attribution and rewards would need to travel with those deliverables through explicit agreements. This is a proposed business direction inferred from the supplier market, not a finding that existing vendors already implement SIP.

This complements the [frontier-lab terms audit](FRONTIER_LAB_IP_AND_CREATOR_REWARDS_AUDIT.md) and the [commercial-platform decision](SOVEREIGN_SOURCE_AI_CODEX_SPEC.md#commercial-platform-decision-and-research). It does not change the selected Nemotron 3 Ultra target or approve any supplier.

## How to read the evidence

**Named primary** means a lab, supplier or research publication identifies a relationship and its scope. A supplier's own account is labeled as such; it is evidence of a public claim, not an independent inspection of its contract. **Reported** means journalism identifies the relationship. **Historical** means evidence concerns a dated project or an arrangement subsequently reported to be changing. **Offering** means a relevant service exists, but this review did not verify a named Claude or ChatGPT training customer. **Retrieval/integration** means access at answer time or a product connection, without establishing permission to train model weights.

No public source reviewed provides a complete, current vendor ledger for either lab. A partnership announcement rarely identifies every model, dataset, price, subcontractor or renewal. A benchmark citation also does not establish that its test examples entered training. Dates below describe the evidence, not guaranteed contract validity on the research date.

The emphasis is US labs and suppliers relevant to the project's US sourcing preference. This is **not a certification of US ownership, US-only labor, US-only content or domestic processing**. Global communities appear as research sources, not automatically eligible suppliers. Workforce location, corporate ownership and data origin require separate verification; model-origin restrictions remain in the existing specification.

| Evaluations and learning environments | Tasks, scoring rubrics, executable checks, tools and simulated workflows | Snorkel, micro1, Prime Intellect, Synopsys | Recognize task authors, reviewers, tool owners and environment maintainers separately. |
| Retrieval and content access | Search results, document access and licensed responses at inference time | Brave, Wiley, TollBit, ProRata | Record actual authorized use; negotiate training separately. |
| Public research infrastructure | Crawl archives, dataset documentation, curation research and standards | Common Crawl, Hugging Face, DataComp, MLCommons | Reuse methods and inspect provenance; public availability is not an admission decision. |

These layers overlap. Buying an expert's labor is different from licensing an existing work; a data platform may perform both. Post-training examples and evaluations can be important purchases without constituting a model's original pretraining corpus.

## A supplier strategy for Sovereign Source

The following is a proposed next-stage shortlist, based on fit with SIP rather than market share. No pricing comparison, purchase, outreach or vendor acceptance has occurred.

| Priority | Candidate set | Concrete next deliverable to request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Permissioned source works | Created by Humans, Defined.ai, direct creator syndicates | A small domain collection with asset-level permission records, attribution fields and an explicit allowed-use schedule. |
| 2. Expert-created training contributions | Surge and Turing; compare Mercor, Handshake and Labelbox | A sample of original tasks, demonstrations and critiques with contributor agreements and independently reviewable quality evidence. |
| 3. Evaluation and environment contributions | Snorkel, micro1 and Prime Intellect's public ecosystem | Reproducible tasks and scoring rules, separated training/test sets, dependency licenses and named contribution roles. |
| 4. Inference-time content economics | TollBit, direct publisher agreements; compare ProRata | A usage receipt and reward statement that can be reconciled to the actual agreement and content delivered. |

For the first pilot, choose one domain and buy a small, inspectable package before negotiating broad volume. Evaluate the data against a held-out task set, inspect the permission chain, and demonstrate a contributor statement. Only then consider a larger purchase or Nemotron adaptation. This is a proposed procurement sequence, not an instruction to start training or spend funds.

### What a SIP supplier agreement should make explicit

These are project requirements to investigate, not a statement that current suppliers offer them:

1. **Authority:** who created the material, who controls each relevant right, and who may grant the proposed permission.
2. **Use:** distinguish retrieval, training, evaluation, synthetic derivatives, tool execution and redistribution of model weights.
3. **Source history:** identify copied material, generated components, source models and applicable terms; preserve the project's model-origin screen.
4. **Contribution roles:** separate source author, task designer, annotator, reviewer, curator and acknowledged influence.
5. **Credit:** agree what can be publicly attributed and what requires a private or pseudonymous record.
6. **Compensation:** distinguish task fees, license fees, usage payments and any continuing royalties; define beneficiary splits and reporting.
7. **Supply chain:** disclose permitted subcontractors, work locations, processing locations and downstream recipients.
8. **Quality:** provide review results, task reproducibility, duplicate checks and separation from evaluation material.
9. **Changes:** specify expiry, withdrawal, corrections and consequences for already delivered data or trained checkpoints; do not promise automatic model unlearning.
10. **Portability and disputes:** export source identifiers, agreement versions and payment records, with a correction and appeal process.

Record inclusion in a training manifest as **training participation**. Record a retrieved asset as **retrieval use**. Keep acknowledged influences and estimated influence separate from both. Neither a supplier invoice nor a similarity score proves how much a particular work caused an answer, and neither should silently determine a creator's payment.

The proposed upstream business is therefore a **syndicate supplying permissioned contributions with auditable agreements and rewards**. Its initial sale could be a licensed collection or an expert task package. Training the project's own model is a separate use requiring its own admission decision, and it does not retrospectively clear the inherited sources of Nemotron Ultra.

## Remaining uncertainties

- Current customer rosters, spend and renewal status are mostly undisclosed. Historical partnerships are not live-contract guarantees.
- The reviewed sources do not establish asset-level attribution or continuing creator royalties across these suppliers. Worker pay and platform licensing revenue are different from such a program.
- US-only sourcing remains a collection- and contract-level question. A US office does not establish where a vendor's contributors or source works originate.
- Private forums, subscriber-only reporting and confidential contracts were not inspected. Their existence is documented only where public entry points support it.
- The report is a discovery map, not a certification of dataset legality, model ancestry, labor practices or vendor security. The next evidence should be samples and agreements for a specific proposed purchase.
