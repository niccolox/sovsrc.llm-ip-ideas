# Sovereign Source startup go-to-market plan

**Planning date: 12 October 2026. Horizon: the next 90 days and 12 months.**

This is a proposed commercial plan for Super Sovereign Source LLM IP. The repository is at specification stage; it does not establish a working product, customers, revenue, funding or an operating team. Targets, prices, conversion rates and budgets below are hypotheses to test. Company-wide traction and available capital remain unverified.

## 1. Executive summary

Start with **US specialist technology research publishers and analyst firms** that own or can license a valuable archive, already sell research, and need their analysts to answer recurring client questions. Sell a research workflow that combines useful answers with source permissions, attribution receipts and agreed contributor compensation. The publisher can be both the first customer and the first source supplier, reducing the need to attract two unrelated sides of a marketplace at launch.

The first commercial offer is a **$5,000, two-week readiness engagement**: inventory a bounded collection, identify permission gaps, measure the existing workflow and define a pilot with acceptance criteria. Once the production gates pass, offer an **eight-week, $15,000 limited pilot**, followed by a proposed **$7,500/month managed subscription**. These are price tests, not an existing price list. Dedicated infrastructure and extensive integrations require separate quotes.

Use founder-led sales, a small set of practical research assets and publisher/community introductions. Assume **$2,000/month in marketing cash**, plus founder time, until the actual budget is confirmed. Engineering, legal review, content acquisition and production operations need separate funding.

The 90-day commercial target is 20 buyer interviews, 10 contributor/rights interviews, six scoped workshops, three signed design-partner scopes and two paid readiness engagements. The two engagements represent **$10,000 in services bookings**, not annual recurring revenue. A production pilot can start only when its gates pass; the production plan estimates weeks 11–16 with a dedicated team. The conditional 12-month base case is six subscription customers and **$540,000 exit ARR**, with substantial execution and pricing uncertainty.

The long-term sequence is: one useful paid workflow → repeatable customer deployments → additional licensed suppliers and syndicates → portable rights and settlement services → separately financed model training. Revenue from early deployments does not establish that a new frontier model is financially feasible.

## 2. Strategic frame

### Positioning and first customer

**Proposed positioning:** Sovereign Source helps specialist research businesses use their archives in AI-assisted research while preserving source permissions, contributor credit and auditable reward agreements.

Lead with faster, verifiable research and manageable rights operations. Explain “Super Intellectual Property” after the buyer understands the job being done: it records formal works, accepted contributions, ideas and acknowledged influences separately, with compensation determined by agreements.

| Selection dimension | First-segment hypothesis | Evidence required to qualify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organization | US specialist technology/engineering publisher or analyst firm; approximately 10–50 researchers/editors | Actual workflow, budget owner and purchasing authority; size is a screening aid, not a hard rule. |
| Content | Approximately 500–20,000 owned or potentially licensable reports/articles | An authorized person can identify rights and third-party exclusions for the pilot subset. |
| Job | Analysts repeatedly search prior research to answer client questions or prepare briefs | Observe recent tasks, time spent, review work and errors. |
| Value | Enough recurring work to justify a $90,000 annual subscription | Buyer accepts a measured annual benefit of at least $180,000, or another documented purchasing rationale. |
| Buying trigger | A funded archive/AI initiative, growing research workload, or a concrete rights-administration problem | A named project with a decision window, not general enthusiasm about AI. |
| Readiness | Willing to start with a limited internal workflow and enrolled sources | Named champion, source administrator, security contact and acceptance owner. |

The economic buyer is likely the CEO, general manager or head of product; the champion is likely research operations or an editorial lead. Contributors and the rights administrator must accept relevant terms. Security and counsel can veto a deployment even when the champion likes the demo. Validate these roles rather than assuming one person controls every decision.

An illustrative value calculation is 150 analyst hours saved per month × an assumed $100 loaded hourly cost = $15,000/month in capacity value. That is not necessarily cash savings. Measure time including verification and corrections; count additional subscription revenue only when observed, and avoid counting the same benefit twice.

Defer general consumer chat, broad news aggregation, government procurement, and clinical/legal decision workflows. Universities and professional associations are potential later segments; frontier labs are potential later licensing buyers. Each has a different purchasing process and should receive its own validated offer.

### Market size and expansion

There is no verified total addressable market in this plan. Do not multiply the global AI market by an arbitrary percentage. Build a deduplicated US account list with content fit, source authority, workflow, current tools and a plausible budget owner. Reconcile parent companies so multiple publications do not become fictitious independent buyers.

Use two bottom-up checks. A proposed annual account program of 150 organizations, 30 qualified opportunities and a 20% opportunity-to-subscription conversion yields six customers. A capacity limit of two concurrent eight-week pilots permits at most 12 starts in 48 operating weeks; six conversions would require 50% of those pilots to convert. Both are planning arithmetic, not observed rates. Content onboarding and engineering capacity can reduce the second limit substantially.

At six customers paying $90,000 annually, the initial obtainable-revenue target is $540,000 ARR. This is an execution target, not a market-size estimate. If a verified list cannot support enough qualified opportunities, revise the segment before increasing marketing spend.

### First assets and message

Create a one-page offer, a source-permission checklist, a sample attribution/reward statement using synthetic parties, and a three-minute workflow demo once a functioning demonstration exists. Mark mockups and simulated payouts clearly. The first research article should explain why source credit, usage permission and payment eligibility are different; the second should show the cost and benefit of a real, permissioned pilot when evidence is available.

**Draft buyer message:** “Your analysts already have valuable research. Sovereign Source is developing a way to answer questions from an approved collection, show supporting sources and administer agreed contributor rewards. We are seeking design partners for a bounded internal workflow. A two-week readiness engagement would establish source permissions, current research effort and the criteria for a paid pilot.”

**Primary call to action:** “Discuss a paid research-archive readiness engagement.” Offer a short fit call before payment. Do not use “Start free” until there is an actual self-service product.

Interview questions should elicit evidence: walk through the last research request; identify who reviewed it; quantify elapsed and staff time; ask which sources were unusable and why; ask what a previous AI trial failed to do; identify the budget and procurement steps; ask what would justify or rule out the proposed fee. Request permission to retain notes and separately to publish quotations. Never manufacture customer wording from these hypotheses.

### Pipeline and sales process

The first-quarter planning funnel is **50 researched accounts → 20 buyer interviews → six qualified workshops → three signed design-partner scopes → two paid readiness engagements**. These imply 40%, 30%, 50% and approximately 67% step conversions, respectively. They are aggressive assumptions for a small, relationship-led cohort, not cold-email benchmarks. Conduct the 10 contributor/rights interviews separately; an individual may have both roles but is counted once in each explicitly defined research cohort.

A qualified opportunity has a recurring task, a budget owner, source authority, a measurable outcome and a decision date. A design-partner scope records commitments and may be unpaid; it is not revenue. A paid engagement requires an executed order and a collection schedule. Record disqualification and lost reasons, not just positive replies.

Sequence: fit call → observed workflow/rights discussion → scoped proposal and cost review → buyer/security review → accepted order → readiness delivery → separate production gate → paid pilot → acceptance review → subscription. Target a four-to-eight-week readiness sales cycle for planning, but measure actual cycle time. No discount substitutes for missing rights or delivery capability.

## 5. Activation

### Activate the buyer

The readiness engagement is a manually deliverable service. Propose a cap of 20 delivery hours and a sample of up to 100 assets from one collection; inventory the wider archive without claiming to have reviewed every item. In week one, identify the champion and rights owner, select the sample, document permitted processing and capture at least 20 representative historical questions. In week two, deliver a rights-gap inventory, workflow baseline, cost model and proposed pilot scope. Use synthetic material until the real material is approved. A readiness engagement does not include a legal opinion or a functioning deployed LLM. Requote additional review work instead of silently expanding the scope.

For a later production pilot, cap the proposed initial scope at one collection, up to 1,000 admitted documents, 25 named internal users and 5,000 answers/month. Confirm token, document-size, concurrency and support limits after measurement; document count alone is not a cost cap. Propose business-hours support and agreed acceptance meetings, with availability commitments backed by the production gates rather than marketing claims.

Define activation as **five named users completing an accepted real task, with valid source receipts, during the first 14 days after production access**. Capture time and reviewer effort against the baseline. The time-to-value clock starts after permissions, access and model admission, while separately reporting the full contracting-to-activation time so onboarding delays remain visible.

The buyer's 20-question sample helps scope the workflow; it does not replace the production plan's larger held-out evaluation, isolation, revocation, accounting and recovery checks. Before production use, the proposed 1,000-task evaluation and all applicable release gates must pass. [Production acceptance plan](PRODUCTION_SYSTEM_PLAN.md)

### Activate supply and solve the marketplace cold start

Start with the paying publisher's cleared collection and a small enrolled set of contributors. An initial planning target is 5–10 contributor agreements relevant to actual pilot questions, not thousands of unreviewed uploads. There is no creator listing fee in the proposed launch offer.

| Supply step | Required artifact | Activation evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identify source authority | Asset inventory and authorized representative | A source can enter review without assuming a publisher owns every embedded asset. |
| Agree uses and beneficiaries | Accepted grant, contribution terms and any syndicate mandate/splits | Permissions and compensation conditions are recorded separately. |
| Demonstrate accounting | Sample statement and an example correction | Contributor understands estimates, earned amounts, reserves and actual payouts. |
| Record eligible use | Accepted delivery, retrieval, execution or adoption evidence under the relevant agreement | A qualifying event can be traced to the rule and beneficiary. |
| Settle when due | Reviewed ledger, payout details and reconciliation | Real payment evidence, or an explicitly labeled simulation before payment launch. |

Contracted retrieval fees or commissioned contributions can compensate early suppliers. Do not describe retrieved text as training exposure. The proposed 60% corpus portion of the creator pool uses actual admitted training exposure; if no eligible training cohort exists, retain the allocation under the agreement's reserve rules. The pilot cannot silently repurpose that share to make early payouts appear larger.

Add an outside supplier only when repeated paid questions reveal a content gap and its rights and acquisition cost are acceptable. Later marketplace listings should begin with demonstrated demand, accepted mandates and an explicit use case. Open-license access must remain available on its original terms without compulsory marketplace participation.

## 6. Retention

Run a weekly pilot review with the champion: completed tasks, sampled answer quality, time saved, unresolved permissions, support effort and cost. Run a monthly rights and contributor review: source changes, expiring grants, disputed credits, earned obligations and reconciled payouts. Tell customers when an answer cannot be supported; unsupported confidence undermines repeat use.

Proposed pilot-to-subscription gates are at least five weekly active users for four consecutive weeks, a buyer-accepted benefit case, no unresolved critical permission or accounting failure, and an agreed operating cost envelope. A user is active only after completing a research task; logins and empty queries do not count. These commercial gates supplement the technical release criteria.

At 30 days without repeated use, diagnose relevance, quality, access friction and workflow fit with the champion. At 60 days without an accepted benefit case, narrow or end the pilot rather than automatically extending it for free. Preserve export and deletion obligations, source revocations, accrued contributor compensation and dispute records through offboarding.

Track customer retention and source participation separately. A retained customer does not prove satisfied contributors; a signed contributor does not prove demand. Report actual renewal cohorts when mature. Do not project lifetime value from a few weeks of pilot activity.

## 7. Referral

After an accepted pilot, ask whether the customer will authorize a case study, introduce one peer, or host a small private demonstration. Obtain permission for names, logos, quotes and confidential performance data. Ask creators for relevant source introductions only after they have seen credible statements and, where due, actual payment.

Start with unpaid introductions. A later referral program could use a fixed fee for an independently qualified, collected sale, with written terms and disclosures. Keep referral compensation outside creator royalties; it must not influence evidence ranking, attribution decisions or syndicate authority. Do not award recurring IP entitlements for merely bringing in another user.

Measure referred qualified opportunities, collected sales, referral cost and cohort retention. A share button or contributor count is not evidence of a functioning referral channel.

### Proposed offer ladder

| Offer | Proposed price | Scope and commercial boundary |
| --- | ---: | --- |

| Readiness engagement | $5,000 fixed | Two weeks, bounded rights/workflow inventory, baseline and pilot recommendation; proposed 50% at start and 50% on accepted delivery. |

Readiness and pilot fees are separate services/limited-term revenue; do not include them in ARR or promise automatic credits against a subscription. If negotiated credits are offered, record their effect on collections and margin. State delay, cancellation, refund and acceptance terms before signature. A deposit for undeliverable future software is not validation of a functioning service.

At the proposed readiness cap, 20 delivery hours at an assumed $100/hour plus a conservative $1,000 reward allocation leaves $2,000 from the $5,000 fee before acquisition, payment fees and other overhead. Time-track the service to test that assumption. Two such engagements can fund learning, not the production team.

The $7,500 price intentionally tests whether this is a high-value workflow. A cheaper shared tier is a later cost and demand experiment, not an advertised launch commitment. If buyers see only generic chatbot value, change the scope or segment rather than concealing delivery losses behind an introductory price.

### Creator economics

For covered revenue under an adopted agreement, the draft reward pool is **20% of eligible collected revenue** after refunds, chargebacks, sales taxes and direct payment processing fees. Compute, content licensing, salaries and general overhead do not reduce that base. Allocate the pool 60% to eligible corpus exposure, 20% to eligible workflow executions and 20% to accepted ideas/contributions/influences, under the specified rules. [Royalty engine](SOVEREIGN_SOURCE_AI_CODEX_SPEC.md#12-royalty-engine)

Retrieval fees, bounties and minimum guarantees are separate obligations; offset only where the agreement permits a specific payment to the same beneficiary to be counted once. Record a reserved allocation as a cost/obligation for planning even if not yet paid out; the reserve is not profit or a claim to a legally constituted escrow. Syndicate administration defaults to zero unless expressly agreed. Do not call the 20% reward pool a marketplace commission or conflate subscription revenue with third-party transaction volume.

Each order must define whether its receipts are covered by the program. The financial scenarios conservatively apply 20% to all illustrated customer collections. They do not carve services out of rewards by assumption. Open-source permissions do not become conditional on paying these commercial fees.

### Cost floor and unit economics

Use the [production plan's illustrative operating costs](PRODUCTION_SYSTEM_PLAN.md#capacity-and-operating-budget) as a stress case: $4,380 GPUs, $240 eligible API usage, $2,000 CPU/database/storage/monitoring and $500 licensed retrieval per month, totaling $7,120 before rewards and people. These are assumptions, not quotes or demonstrated capacity. Reprice if the admitted model route, workload or concurrency changes.

At six customers, add an assumed $200/customer/month of direct delivery support, giving **$8,320 in non-pool delivery cost**. That support allowance is optimistic and needs time tracking. It is allocated labor, not evidence that a full support hire costs $1,200/month. Do not double-count it when reconciling the staffing budget.

| Monthly run-rate scenario | Eligible collections | 20% pool | Other delivery cost | Contribution after these costs | Indicative margin |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One customer, whole illustrative fleet | $7,500 | $1,500 | $7,320 | -$1,320 | -17.6% |
| Six customers at $3,000 each | $18,000 | $3,600 | $8,320 | $6,080 | 33.8% |
| Six customers at proposed $7,500 each | $45,000 | $9,000 | $8,320 | $27,680 | 61.5% |

For clarity, these scenarios assume no refunds, taxes or processing deductions, so illustrated receipts equal eligible revenue. Actual payment fees and other omitted delivery costs reduce margin. This is a contribution model, not reported GAAP gross margin or profit; central product engineering, sales, counsel and company overhead remain outside it. One customer cannot justify the full fleet on these assumptions without deliberate funded learning costs.

With $8,320 in non-pool cost and a 20% pool, a 60% contribution margin needs at least **$41,600/month** in eligible revenue: `0.8 − 8,320 / revenue >= 0.6`. This explains why low pricing should wait for a measured cheaper delivery design. It also requires that the modeled fleet actually support the workload.

For acquisition planning, $24,000 annual marketing cash plus 360 founder hours at an assumed $100/hour gives $60,000. At six new subscription customers, that is a conservative **$10,000 GTM cost per acquired customer**, or $4,000 in cash. It includes some retention/content overhead and excludes paid delivery time; replace it with acquisition-only cohort CAC when records exist. At the six-customer contribution level, $10,000 divided by $4,613/customer/month is about **2.2 months**. This conditional payback arithmetic is not an achieved CAC or a reason to buy ads before repeatable sales.

These are roles to assign, not claims that people have been hired. One founder can own early interviews and sales, but cannot silently substitute for all production/security/finance responsibilities.

Review pipeline and interviews weekly; reconcile collections, delivery cost and contributor obligations monthly; revisit segment and price after the first six qualified workshops; revisit the full plan each quarter. Tag lead source and ask buyers how they found the project. Use first-touch, latest meaningful touch and buyer-reported discovery without pretending to know exact causal attribution from a small sample.

The founder must resolve five open decisions before execution spending: available cash/runway and time, first subsegment, legal entity and contracting capacity, who delivers readiness and production work, and the accepted model/provider admission path. The recommended prices and scenario budget remain proposals until adopted. Publishing this plan does not spend funds, contact prospects, sign supplier terms or launch a product.

### Claims and launch boundaries

Sales can describe the proposed workflow and documented research. Demonstrations must state what is implemented, manual or simulated. Do not claim an infringement-free model, universal rights clearance, automatic ownership of ideas, guaranteed earnings or causal attribution of every generated token. Ideas and influences are recognized and rewarded through evidence and agreements; SIP does not create new statutory rights.

The custom SS-SIP draft is not OSI approved and is not automatically applied to the whole repository. A future open-source SDK or receipt format needs its own explicit licensing decision. [Licensing guide](../licenses/README.md)

US model origin does not establish the absence of Chinese learned lineage. The current research has not qualified a general frontier teacher under the strict screen, and the selected Nemotron 3 Ultra has a disclosed conflict.
