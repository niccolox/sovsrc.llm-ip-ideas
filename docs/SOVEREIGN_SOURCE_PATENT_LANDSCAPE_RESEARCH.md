# Patents relevant to Sovereign Source AI

**Project:** Super Sovereign Source LLM IP  
**Research date:** October 15, 2026  
**Scope:** US patents and published applications concerning AI source attribution, training-data valuation, licensed generation, contributor payments and collaborative marketplaces.

**Sovereign Source is entering an already patented field.** The most relevant findings include Sureel's granted attribution-and-compensation patents, Technica Pacifica's granted conditional data-use patent, Preferred Networks' pending retrieval-and-royalty application, and BRIA's pending textual and chained-attribution applications. They warrant review against the actual implementation; they do not establish that one company owns the general idea of rewarding contributors. The comparison below identifies the specific published claims supporting this assessment.

Keep the proposed pilot focused on permissioned retrieval, recorded source use and accepted contributor agreements. That remains a practical engineering choice, but **retrieval with rewards is not an established patent safe harbor**. Obtain a claim-by-claim freedom-to-operate assessment before commercial deployment. This landscape is a research screen, not a legal opinion on infringement, validity or patentability.

Related project documents: [LLM Rights and Content Awards standard](LLM_RIGHTS_AND_CONTENT_AWARDS_SPEC.md), [project specification](SOVEREIGN_SOURCE_AI_CODEX_SPEC.md), [licensed-content billing research](LICENSED_CONTENT_PREMIUM_TOKEN_BILLING_RESEARCH.md) and [research LLM stack](OPEN_SOURCE_RESEARCH_LLM_STACK.md).

## How to read the comparison

The shortlist contains **12 selected US documents: eight grants and four published applications**. Several belong to related portfolios; these are not twelve independent patent families. Priority reflects relevance to the proposed features, not an infringement probability or a ranking of patent strength.

The linked records reproduce patent publications and their claims. “Active,” “pending,” assignee names and earliest priority dates are the **Google Patents index's reported values on the research date**. They have not been certified against USPTO prosecution files, assignment records, maintenance fees, terminal disclaimers or litigation. An earliest family priority date does not establish that every claim is entitled to that date. For granted applications, the comparison uses the grant's claims rather than assuming the earlier application claims survived unchanged.

An **independent claim** specifies a claimed combination without incorporating another claim. A **dependent claim** adds requirements to the claim it incorporates. Similarity to a title or abstract is insufficient: infringement analysis compares the claims with the product or process. An application publication is not a granted patent, although qualifying pre-grant activity can create provisional royalty exposure after issuance under specific conditions. [USPTO on patent rights and infringement](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/manage), [USPTO on application publication and provisional rights](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/apply).

US patents can be relevant regardless of their owner's nationality. The project's preference for US-origin models does not exclude US patent rights held by foreign organizations. This review includes such rights as legal and technical research, not as supplier recommendations. [USPTO on territorial rights and foreign applicants](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/essentials).

## The closest overlaps with the planned platform

### Retrieval and contributor payments

Preferred Networks' published claims are the clearest reason not to equate a retrieval-based architecture with patent clearance. Claim 1 does not require estimating which training example caused an answer. Claims 2–6 add usage counts, contribution measures, royalty records and provider reporting; claim 33 separately addresses reporting information use. Changing the product term from “royalty” to “Content Award” does not itself change the implemented process. [Published claims](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20260228313A1/en).

The commercial decision should therefore include examination of the current prosecution record, any amendments and related filings. A negotiated fixed fee or alternative billing arrangement may be commercially useful, but this report does not establish it as a legal workaround.

### Estimated influence and actual use

The two Sureel grants cover different technical routes: comparing model changes during training and comparing generated outputs with representations of source material. They are not blanket claims to every attribution record. However, adding a similarity-to-payment pipeline could make their detailed limitations much more relevant than they are to a ledger recording explicit source IDs. The comparison should cover every relevant independent claim and family member. [Model-based grant](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12013891B2/en), [output-based grant](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12314308B2/en).

SIP already distinguishes retrieved support, training participation, acknowledged influence and estimated influence. Preserve those distinctions in the API, user interface and reward agreements. They make the evidence understandable and support a precise technical comparison; they do not establish noninfringement.

### Upstream contributions through generated material

BRIA's chained-attribution application closely resembles the ambition to recognize an upstream source even when an intermediate generated work is reused. Its dependent claims describe secondary attribution to the training examples behind generated auxiliary content. A voluntary, human-approved influence acknowledgment and this automated analysis are different mechanisms to document separately. [Claims 41 and 49–54](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20260093745A1/en).

### Model instructions that carry rights conditions

Technica Pacifica's grant is especially relevant if source-rights identifiers become part of what the model learns and emits. Its claim 1 includes training, an identifier in intermediate output, retrieval of the linked condition and response modification. Claim 2 adds payment; blockchain recording appears in dependent claim 3, so omitting blockchain alone does not dispose of claims 1–2. [Granted claims](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12657517B1/en).

For the pilot, keep source identity and permission evidence in the retrieval and authorization system. Do not assume a generated identifier is authoritative evidence of rights or use. This preserves the existing security boundary while giving counsel a clear implementation to assess.

## Architecture implications

The following are engineering recommendations inferred from the claim comparison and the existing project design. They are not confirmed design-arounds.

| Platform component | Recommended approach | Patent question to resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permission checks | Keep Rust/Axum and reviewed policies authoritative. Pydantic AI can propose evidence for review, but cannot issue rights or authorize payments. | Compare this implementation with Citibank's agent-driven permission generation and stored artifacts. |
| Retrieval and source receipts | Record actual fetched assets, passage IDs, agreement versions and qualified-use events outside the model. | Chart Preferred Networks' retrieval, payment and provider-reporting claims against the complete service. |
| Training provenance | Preserve admitted-source manifests, license scope and training-run evidence. | Determine whether proposed additions measure model-parameter changes, train an attribution model or emit learned source-condition identifiers. |
| Attribution research | Keep experimental influence estimates behind a replaceable interface. Label method, uncertainty and what the evidence establishes. | Review the selected algorithm against Sureel, BRIA, Adobe and BG Negev/Fujitsu claims before integrating it into production. |
| Content Awards | Calculate accepted obligations and syndicate splits deterministically, with an auditable ledger and duplicate-event protection. | Assess the whole combination of attribution, triggering event and payment; changing a label or moving settlement to another service is not clearance. |
| Ideas and influences | Use accepted acknowledgments and explicit participation agreements. Preserve the difference between credit and a payable obligation. | Compare automated multi-stage attribution separately from human-reviewed contribution relationships. |
| Syndicate marketplace | Start with source licenses, commissions and contribution agreements. Treat distributed collaborative model training as a separate feature. | Review Addepalli's model transfer/training combination and Huawei's model-tested dataset valuation if those features are introduced. |
| Portable exports | Prefer a documented receipt format with evidence references; evaluate embedded or inferred attribution as optional features. | Review BRIA's marking and downstream usage applications if the export and metering flow adopts those steps. |

The choice of Python, Pydantic, Prefect, Rust, Axum, PostgreSQL or Nemotron does not by itself answer these questions. The relevant comparison is what the combined system does. Purchasing model inference, licensing training data and obtaining rights to an attribution component are separate transactions.

## Implications for the open standard and SIP license

Keep the core LLM Rights and Content Awards standard focused on interoperable records, evidence semantics and observable accounting behavior. Avoid making one proprietary influence algorithm a mandatory conformance requirement. An implementation should be able to explain which evidence it uses without pretending all methods produce interchangeable causal measurements.

Before organizing formal standardization, create an explicit policy for participant patent disclosures, essential-claim licensing commitments, exclusions and replacement of encumbered mandatory mechanisms. W3C's policy offers a relevant model: it seeks royalty-free implementation through defined commitments by participants. The project's independent W3C-style draft does not receive those commitments merely by copying the format. [W3C Patent Policy](https://www.w3.org/policies/patent-policy/).

The proposed SIP license should distinguish permission to use contributed software or content from any patent grant. A contributor can only grant rights it controls; an open-source release cannot clear an unrelated holder's patent. Any contributor patent clause should receive legal review and identify the covered claims and implementation scope.

**Content rewards and patent royalties are separate.** A royalty-free interoperability standard can still support payments to authors, researchers and other contributors under source-use agreements. Conversely, paying a source contributor does not automatically license a third party's patented attribution method.

## Potential Sovereign Source invention work

The broad ambition to attribute AI outputs and reward upstream contributors is already heavily represented in the publications above. A future application would need a specific technical invention and a defensible comparison with earlier work. The present research does not establish novelty for any feature.

Useful topics for invention review include a concrete protocol that prevents duplicate liabilities across overlapping syndicates; privacy-preserving audit evidence that can be verified across sovereign deployments; and a precise mechanism for changing permissions while preserving historically accrued obligations. For each, record the technical problem, implemented mechanism, measured improvement, alternatives and human inventors. These are research directions, not recommendations to assert broad ownership over ideas or rewards.

The repository and website already disclose parts of the design. Assemble the actual public-release dates and contents before deciding whether to file. The US has a limited inventor-disclosure grace-period framework, while many countries can deny protection when disclosure precedes filing; do not assume GitHub publication preserves worldwide options. [USPTO on disclosure and international filing](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/international-protection/filing-patents-abroad).

## Review priorities before commercialization

1. **Create a concrete implementation claim chart.** Start with Preferred Networks for retrieval payments; Technica Pacifica for learned rights identifiers; Sureel for influence-based compensation; and BRIA for textual and upstream attribution. Record each limitation as present, absent or unresolved, with evidence from the intended design.
2. **Verify official records.** Use USPTO Patent Center for prosecution, amended claims and continuations; verify assignments, maintenance and any validity proceedings. Inspect all relevant independent claims rather than relying on this selection.
3. **Define the operating geography and actors.** Identify where training, retrieval, authorization and settlement occur, which entity performs each step, and where the service is supplied. A US-focused shortlist is not global clearance.
4. **Evaluate supplier coverage.** For an attribution or licensing vendor, request patent numbers, license scope, customer and downstream rights, exclusions, indemnity terms and the effect of modifications or self-hosting. A SaaS subscription alone should not be assumed to cover every integration.
5. **Choose a documented commercial route.** Depending on the completed analysis, that may mean a licensed component, a negotiated patent license, a genuinely different implementation or a narrowed feature set. Have patent counsel assess the result before making noninfringement claims.

## Search scope and limitations

Searches combined terms for training-data attribution, creator compensation, AI royalties, licensed data exchange, chained attribution, model marketplaces and dataset incentives. Assignee and related-document searches included Sureel, BRIA, Adobe, ProRata and adjacent providers. Published claim text and document metadata were inspected, with application-to-grant and selected US-family follow-up. Legal interpretation references use USPTO and W3C primary guidance; technical background uses the original research publications.

This is a targeted landscape, not an exhaustive classification search, complete family review or official legal-status audit. Unpublished applications and unindexed documents can be absent. General transformer, training, model-compression and deployment patents are outside this focused review. Branding and trademark clearance for Sovereign Source are also outside its scope.
