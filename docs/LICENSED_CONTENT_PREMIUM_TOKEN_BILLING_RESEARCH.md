# AI data brokers and licensed-token billing

**Project:** Super Sovereign Source LLM IP

**Research date:** October 14, 2026

**Scope:** Content suppliers, licensing marketplaces and payment infrastructure for LLMs; existing token-based products and a proposed Sovereign Source billing model.

**Yes: licensed-token billing already exists. Bigdata.com is the closest direct commercial example found in this review, and Redpine also sells licensed research access by token.** The opportunity for Sovereign Source is to combine this purchasing model with explicit contributor and syndicate agreements, source receipts and auditable rewards. A content token is a billing unit; its associated agreement determines the rights purchased. [Bigdata.com launch](https://bigdata.com/resources/bigdata-com-launches-the-tokenization-of-content), [Redpine pricing](https://www.redpine.ai/pricing).

For the initial product, offer **licensed retrieval with separate compute and content charges**. Add a premium token bundle after measuring source costs and customer demand. Procure model-training rights separately: several attractive retrieval services expressly restrict training, caching or redistribution.

This is a research recommendation, not a product launch or a change to the project's license or reward policy. The comparison includes international suppliers as commercial benchmarks. It does not qualify their jurisdictions, infrastructure or underlying models against the project's US-model and lineage requirements. Broader supplier coverage remains in the [ten data brokers report](FRONTIER_AI_DATA_BROKERS_TOP_10_REPORT.md).

## What a licensed token can mean

The same word describes several different things. Keep their meters and permissions separate.

| Unit | What it measures or authorizes | Meaning for Sovereign Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model input/output token | Text processed or generated by an LLM's tokenizer. | Compute usage. A compute bill alone does not establish permission for source content. |
| Licensed source token | A measured portion of content supplied under a specified license. | Can support content pricing when tied to a source, tokenizer, permitted use and receipt. |
| Training exposure token | A token processed during an authorized training run, potentially across repeated passes. | A possible contractual meter; distinguish unique corpus size from repeated training exposure. |
| Access token | An authentication or authorization credential. | TollBit's tokens are JWT access grants, not LLM vocabulary tokens or a cryptocurrency. [Documentation](https://docs.tollbit.com/docs/tokens) |
| Credit | A prepaid balance or commercial allowance. | Its conversion into dollars, requests or tokens must be stated. It does not itself grant content rights. |
| Payment token | A unit or credential on a payment network. | Payment infrastructure can settle a bill; licensing and beneficiary entitlements still need agreements. |

Retrieved source text normally becomes part of the model's input. Thus the same passage can legitimately incur a content-access charge and an inference-compute charge. Those are separate services, not two independent categories of text. Also, a supplier's tokenizer may produce a different count from the model's tokenizer.

## What the published token prices actually buy

### Bigdata.com: the closest billing precedent, with restrictive default rights

Bigdata.com's July/August 2026 explanation lists approximately **$8/M Standard Tokens** for search/retrieval compute and **$8–$98/M Licensed Tokens**, varying by source. It gives premium-text examples around $36–$74/M. These are supplier-side consumption prices, not a quote for the final LLM's generated output. Its current pricing page also offers content-set subscriptions that remove that set's per-token content charge while retaining standard retrieval charges. [Token pricing explanation](https://bigdata.com/blog/understanding-token-based-usage), [current plans](https://bigdata.com/pricing).

The standard terms use Bigdata.com's own tokenizer. Sections 4.2, 4.5 and 4.6 limit use to single-inference grounding, prohibit retaining the underlying content, require zero-data-retention operation, and restrict training, fine-tuning and evaluation. Training requires written confirmation; third-party product integration requires express authorization, and competing-product restrictions also apply. Section 9 does not establish the customer IP indemnity advertised in the pricing blog; obtain that protection in the governing contract. **Treat this as a pricing benchmark and conditional procurement lead, not an already-approved input to a public Sovereign Source SaaS or training pipeline.** [Terms, sections 1, 4 and 9](https://bigdata.com/terms-and-conditions).

The vendor's claim of up to 100-fold savings compares selected passages with much larger contexts. It does not establish that total workload costs, including licensing, output generation and retries, fall by that amount. Measure the complete cost per accepted answer. [Launch explanation](https://bigdata.com/resources/bigdata-com-launches-the-tokenization-of-content).

### Redpine: genuine content metering, with source-specific rights

The advertised starting rate of $1 per 1,000 tokens normalizes to **$1,000 per million tokens**. That arithmetic does not make it comparable in value to a general LLM's output price. Redpine's preview/unlock API exposes a token count and price for each retrieved result, allowing a buyer to purchase selected passages. Its terminology therefore concerns content returned by the grounding service; it should not be billed as though it were the final assistant's generated answer. [Pricing](https://www.redpine.ai/pricing), [preview and unlock](https://docs.redpine.ai/docs/preview-unlock).

Redpine contracts through a Swedish entity. Its API terms make permissions content-specific: training, storage, external use and other rights must be expressly allowed. They preserve attribution and restrict unapproved training, bulk extraction and competing uses. A custom Access Order can alter specified terms. The advertised research catalog includes Wanfang Data, so origin-sensitive procurement needs collection-level review rather than approval of the entire catalog. [API terms](https://legal.redpine.ai/api-terms), [catalog and launch partners](https://www.redpine.ai/).

### Valyu: a hybrid meter does not imply a training license

Valyu's pricing combines a source-dependent retrieval charge with a token charge for answer synthesis. The $12/M synthesis price is not disclosed as a creator royalty. Its platform agreement permits application use of outputs but excludes training, fine-tuning, pretraining, embedding generation and dataset construction from the output grant. Restricted publisher content has strict retention limits, and output resale/sublicensing is restricted. Obtain an order covering the intended customer-facing service and every required downstream use. [Pricing](https://www.valyu.ai/pricing), [platform agreement, sections 3–7](https://www.valyu.ai/valyu-platform-service-agreement).

### Cloudflare: downstream-use rewards now have a concrete implementation

Pay Per Use lets buyers define a payable use and price, which publishers accept. Buyers submit usage records; Cloudflare bills and settles. This is closer to a content-rewards program than charging for every crawl. However, usage is self-reported, and the September beta does not demonstrate automatic discovery of every influence inside model weights. Sovereign Source would still need reliable event capture and audits. [September 30 announcement](https://blog.cloudflare.com/pay-per-use/).

## Recommended Sovereign Source commercial model

Use **Licensed Context** as the first customer-facing product description. An optional **Creator Rewards** plan can fund the project's agreed revenue pool. “Licensed tokens” can remain a metering term, with the source scope explained wherever the price appears.

| Product | Customer bill | Supplier/contributor settlement | Recommended timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Licensed retrieval | Model compute, content usage in the supplier's native unit, and a disclosed service fee. | Actual licensed access/use fees, plus any separately agreed reward obligations. | Launch first; easiest to reconcile. |
| Premium context bundle | Fixed monthly amount plus a stated allowance of source tokens from an eligible catalog; explicit overage rates. | Preserve actual source-level charges even when the customer sees a bundle. | After workload and source-mix measurements. |
| Creator Rewards plan | A disclosed portion of eligible product revenue funds contracted contributions. | Existing SIP pool policy where expressly selected, or another accepted formula. | Pilot alongside retrieval with consented contributors. |
| Training corpus agreement | Dataset fee, minimum guarantee, measured training exposure or a negotiated model-revenue share. | Payments follow the training grant and agreed descendant obligations. | Separate procurement and research phase. |

The customer buys access, permitted use, source evidence, freshness and service quality. A higher token price should have a concrete explanation: source category, exclusivity, verification, updates, service commitment or reward funding. Price alone is not proof of quality.

For a premium context bundle, define the tokenizer and eligible sources in advance. Do not silently convert every input token into a paid content token: system prompts, user text, tools, licensed passages and generated outputs have different origins. Maintain both the supplier's billed quantity and the model's compute quantity.

Avoid one unlimited price across all content. A bundle calibrated to inexpensive sources can lose money when an agent repeatedly requests costly scientific passages or expert interviews. Use catalog tiers, per-request spending limits, previews and committed-volume negotiations. Redpine's priced preview is a useful design reference; Bigdata.com's content-set subscription illustrates a route for repeated use. [Preview mechanism](https://docs.redpine.ai/docs/preview-unlock), [content subscriptions](https://bigdata.com/pricing).

## Illustrative economics

The following is a **proposed scenario, not a vendor quotation, measured margin or approved price**. It assumes one million billable licensed-context tokens from an explicitly limited catalog, with associated compute and service costs bundled into the displayed price.

| Item per one million licensed-context tokens | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Eligible collected revenue after the permitted deductions | $40.00 |
| Contracted source-access fees | $16.00 |
| Model inference expense | $3.00 |
| Retrieval, verification and other variable delivery expense | $5.00 |
| SIP creator pool: 20% of eligible revenue | $8.00 |
| Remaining contribution toward fixed costs and profit | **$8.00** |

The $8 pool allocates $4.80 to the corpus track, $1.60 to prompts/workflows and $1.60 to ideas/contributions/influences under the existing 60/20/20 policy. Those allocations require eligible agreements and scorecards. Empty tracks remain reserved for reconciliation; retrieval purchases do not automatically qualify as training exposure.

With variable supplier and delivery costs `C`, pool fraction `p`, and target contribution margin `m`, the required eligible revenue is:

```text
R = C / (1 - p - m)

Example: C = $24, p = 0.20, m = 0.20
R = $24 / 0.60 = $40
```

The denominator must be positive. The invoice must also cover whatever deductions separate billed amounts from eligible collected revenue. Fixed operating costs, taxes on profit and working-capital needs are outside this simplified contribution calculation.

If source fees double to $32 while other variable costs remain $8, keeping revenue at $40 produces an $8 loss after the pool. Preserving the same contribution margin requires approximately $66.67 of eligible revenue. Source mix and use frequency therefore matter more than an attractive headline token price.

This example **adds** source fees and the creator pool. It does not deduct source fees or compute before calculating the pool, and it assumes no contractual offset between them. Any permitted offset must be recorded once, at the appropriate contributor or contribution level. A fee already containing a supplier's creator payment must be identified so the additional SIP reward is explained accurately.

## Paying training contributors, ideas and upstream influences

Per-token charging is straightforwardest when the service can identify the purchased source passage. Training is different: corpus inclusion and recorded training exposure are observable, while the origin of every later generated token is not reliably established by a citation or similarity score alone.

For training, negotiate a corpus manifest, allowed purposes, permitted models, evaluation use, repeated-pass accounting, synthetic derivatives, checkpoint distribution, termination treatment and reward obligations. A proposed per-million-training-token price must specify whether it counts deduplicated content once or every exposure. Do not apply an inference-only price to bulk training and assume the rights carry over.

Created by Humans explicitly offers selectable training rights; the prior [broker comparison](FRONTIER_AI_DATA_BROKERS_TOP_10_REPORT.md) also covers Defined.ai and Protege as corpus-procurement leads. Their suitability depends on the particular dataset and agreement. A separate [distillation assessment](DISTILLATION_AS_A_SERVICE_MARKET_RESEARCH.md) covers teacher permissions: purchasing source material does not resolve restrictions imposed by a hosted teacher. [Created by Humans licensing](https://www.createdbyhumans.ai/).

Sovereign Source can reward an idea or upstream influence through a reviewed adoption agreement or an accepted beneficiary split, even when it is not a separately purchased passage. This is a contractual recognition mechanism. It does not create ownership of an abstract idea or prove causality from a model's generated text.

Preserve the current [SIP specification](SOVEREIGN_SOURCE_AI_CODEX_SPEC.md) and [marketplace terms](../licenses/SYNDICATE-MARKETPLACE-TERMS-0.1.md):

- The 20% pool and 60/20/20 allocation apply only when an agreement selects that policy.
- Eligible revenue deducts refunds, chargebacks, sales taxes and directly charged payment processing, not compute or general operating costs.
- Minimum guarantees and agreed per-use fees remain payable even if they exceed the pool.
- Syndicate membership, ownership, attribution and economic shares remain separate records.
- Open-license use does not require marketplace membership or a royalty. Private conversations need separate enrollment consent.

## Proposed implementation in the existing stack

Keep source permissions and accounting outside the LLM. The model may propose relevant passages and citations; the service decides whether a use is allowed and how it is metered.

1. **Offer and permissions:** Python/Pydantic validates source offers, rights-holder authority, license versions, purposes, territories, retention limits, rates and beneficiary schedules. A marketplace listing is not sufficient evidence of every right needed.
2. **Budget and admission:** Rust/Axum authorizes the requested use and reserves its spending limit before paid retrieval. Reject incompatible retention or training conditions before content enters a persistent index.
3. **Meter and evidence:** Record supplier transaction ID, source/version ID, permitted use, original billing unit, quantity, price, currency, tokenizer version where relevant, and the model's independent usage counts. Preserve only evidence the license allows; a receipt or permitted hash can replace prohibited raw-text retention.
4. **Answer and attribution:** Pydantic AI assembles supported answers from admitted sources. Record separately what was purchased, supplied to the model, and cited. Payment follows the agreed event, so purchased but uncited material may still be payable.
5. **Accrual and delivery:** PostgreSQL commits deduplicated usage and payable accruals with a transactional outbox before releasing the licensed result. Failures and retries must not silently duplicate customer charges or supplier settlements.
6. **Reconciliation and payouts:** Prefect reconciles provider invoices, collected revenue, reserves, refunds and split schedules. Use exact decimal/rational calculations, deterministic rounding and immutable adjustment entries. Accrual, external payout initiation and confirmed payment are distinct states.

Cache hits need their own rule. Reduced model-compute cost does not automatically eliminate a content-use fee. Conversely, a subscription or reuse grant must not be charged again as a new content purchase. Retention prohibitions apply to logs, traces and debugging systems as well as the main database.

The auditable deliverable is a source-and-license receipt linked to the answer and a financial ledger linked to the eligible event. Avoid claiming that every generated token is licensed or attributed if the pretrained model's broader training sources have not been cleared.

## Procurement decision and pilot

**Recommended sequence:** use direct syndicate agreements plus TollBit as retrieval procurement candidates; study Bigdata.com's source-level token pricing; compare Redpine for a tightly scoped science catalog; evaluate Cashmere for publisher operations and Cloudflare for use-based settlement. ProRata and Microsoft PCM are potential distribution partnerships. These are evaluation priorities, subject to contracts and technical checks.

Start with one industry-knowledge syndicate and a small consented catalog. Keep financial research as a later phase, consistent with the [SaaS business plan](SAAS_MARKETS_AND_SYNDICATES_BUSINESS_PLAN.md). Financial data adds source-specific display, redistribution and end-user requirements that a generic token rate cannot resolve.

Before supplier selection, obtain a written schedule answering ten questions:

| Decision | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Authority | Who owns or can license each included asset, and what is excluded? |
| 2. Purpose | Does the grant cover grounding, embeddings, evaluation, training and synthetic derivatives separately? |
| 3. Customer use | May Sovereign Source serve its paying customers, display extracts and distribute permitted outputs? |
| 4. Meter | Which tokenizer or native unit, which event, and how are cache hits and retries handled? |
| 5. Price | Source rates, platform fees, minimums, discounts, revisions and spending caps. |
| 6. Retention | What may be cached, indexed, logged or retained as audit evidence, and for how long? |
| 7. Attribution | Required notices, identifiers, citation placement and correction process. |
| 8. Rewards | Payee, revenue basis, split, statement detail, payment timing and any permitted offsets. |
| 9. Sovereignty | Contracting entity, processing regions, subprocessors, source origins and model dependencies. |
| 10. Exit and remedies | Exportable records, deletion obligations, surviving rights, withdrawal, indemnity and liability terms. |

Compare the licensed catalog with a permitted baseline on the same tasks. Measure answer correctness, citation support, freshness, cost per accepted answer, provider-fee reconciliation and contributor earnings. Include paid retrievals that fail to improve an answer, retries and agent loops in costs. Pilot a transparent usage bill before promising a flat premium-token price.

The commercial proposition is credible when the customer can see **what content was licensed, what use was allowed, what was charged and how the agreed rewards were allocated**. The reviewed market supplies useful components; the project's additional contribution would be portable syndicate agreements and consistent accounting across retrieval, training, workflows and adopted ideas.

## Evidence limits

This review uses public vendor pricing, documentation, announcements and terms accessed October 14, 2026. It does not include signed enterprise agreements, private rate cards, authenticated catalogs, test purchases or audited payout statements. Advertised licensing, indemnity, quality and savings claims require verification in the actual order and pilot. No provider account was enrolled and no content was purchased during this research.
