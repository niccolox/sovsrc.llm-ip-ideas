# National sovereign AI projects and commercial national champions

Research date: **12 October 2026**. Prepared for **Super Sovereign Source LLM IP**.

**The strongest comparators for Sovereign Source are Mistral and Cohere for commercial delivery, Switzerland's Apertus for transparent public research, and HUMAIN, G42 and Qai for state-scale infrastructure partnerships.** They solve different parts of sovereignty. Domestic hosting, model ownership, independent development, cultural representation and contributor compensation require separate evidence.

This review covers Canada, Australia, France, Germany, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar and Egypt in depth, with selected comparisons from Switzerland, the UK, India, Singapore, Japan, South Korea and the US. It is a landscape assessment, not a benchmark ranking or complete national inventory. Primary government, university, developer and company sources support the findings below. Provider claims are identified as such; announced investment and future capacity are not counted as delivered infrastructure.

For sovsrc, the opportunity is to supply **permissioned national knowledge collections, attribution evidence and contractual content rewards** to sovereign AI operators. None of the reviewed program descriptions establishes a general, auditable marketplace that pays individual contributors for training participation, qualifying inference use and documented upstream influence. This is a finding about the reviewed public evidence, not proof that these organizations have no private content agreements.

## What counts as sovereign AI?

Four organizational patterns recur:

1. **Public research and infrastructure:** universities, institutes and government facilities develop models or provide compute. Examples include the Swiss AI Initiative, TII and QCRI.
2. **State-owned commercial companies:** a sovereign fund owns an operating business that invests across the stack. HUMAIN and Qai are clear examples.
3. **Private national champions:** a commercial company supplies strategically important domestic models, infrastructure or government services. Cohere, Mistral, Maincode and SoftBank illustrate different stages of this pattern.
4. **Foreign technology partnerships:** a country hosts capacity and purchases services from an international provider. Such arrangements can improve local access while leaving important model and operating decisions with the supplier.

Here, **de facto national project** is an analytical description of a company's strategic role, not a legal designation or claim of state ownership. An ambitious domestic startup and a government-selected national supplier should not receive the same evidentiary status.

### Ten parameters for assessment

| Parameter | Evidence to request |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Ownership and governance | Legal entities, controlling owners, governing jurisdiction and reserved decision rights |
| 2. Compute and operational control | Facility operator, administrators, encryption keys, update authority, backup access and disconnected operation |
| 3. Model control | Exact downloadable artifacts, ability to adapt and serve them, and rights surviving supplier exit |
| 4. Development lineage | Initial weights, continued pretraining, teachers, synthetic datasets, tokenizers and material learned components |
| 5. Data authority | Source-level permission records, privacy basis, acquisition evidence and community authority |
| 6. Commercial freedom | Exact model license and service contract; training, distillation, redistribution and external customer permissions |
| 7. Language and public value | Relevant language, dialect and domain evaluations; local research and public-service access |
| 8. Funding and delivery | Public appropriation versus private commitment; award, construction, commissioning and actual usable capacity |
| 9. Attribution and contributor economics | Source receipts, beneficiary identity, accepted splits, reward rules, settlements and appeals |
| 10. Portability and resilience | Exportable datasets, models and ledgers; alternate suppliers; hardware, software and energy dependencies |

These are procurement questions, not numerical scores inferred from marketing pages. A national brand does not establish source permissions or training ancestry. Conversely, foreign components do not automatically prevent meaningful local operational control: the dependencies and retained authority must be explicit.

## Canada: public compute and a commercial model champion

Canada's **AI for All** strategy explicitly combines domestic compute, research, procurement and company growth. It identifies Cohere and the Montréal nonprofit LawZero as strategic AI organizations. LawZero belongs in the research and safety category; it should not be treated as another commercial enterprise model vendor. [Canada's national strategy](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en/canadas-national-artificial-intelligence-strategy-ai-all).

The **C$2 billion Sovereign AI Compute Strategy**, originating in Budget 2024, allocates up to C$700 million to commercial compute, C$1 billion to public supercomputing and C$300 million to compute access. The current SCIP page describes approximately **C$890 million** for its infrastructure layer over seven fiscal years beginning 2026–27; its application period closed on **1 June 2026**. Do not add overlapping program amounts together or interpret the closed intake as a completed supercomputer. [Compute strategy](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en/canadian-sovereign-ai-compute-strategy), [SCIP status and funding](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en/ai-sovereign-compute-infrastructure-program).

Cohere illustrates why ownership needs to be examined layer by layer. Canada's public statement describes up to **C$240 million** supporting a C$725 million project, with Cohere as anchor tenant in a Cambridge, Ontario facility **built and operated by CoreWeave**. Canadian model capability and Canadian-located compute do not mean Cohere owns and operates the building. [Government project statement, updated 2 September 2026](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en/strategic-response-fund-cohere-compute-expansion-project-public-statement).

Cohere separately documents cloud and on-premises deployment options, including air-gapped environments. These make it a relevant enterprise delivery comparator, but do not establish permission to use every hosted output to train a competing model. [Cohere deployment documentation](https://docs.cohere.com/docs/deployment-options-overview).

**Sovsrc implication:** investigate a Canadian publisher or university consortium as a source-governance pilot. Compute funding, deployment procurement and content-rights agreements would be distinct workstreams. Eligibility for Canadian programs requires its own review.

## Australia: domestic capability through several private suppliers

Australia's **National AI Plan**, launched on **2 December 2025**, supports domestic capability, infrastructure, adoption and safety. Its structure is an ecosystem program; it is not evidence that Australia has selected a single national foundation-model monopoly. [Official plan](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/national-ai-plan).

**Maincode / Matilda** is an emerging commercial comparator. Maincode says it trains its models on its own hardware in Melbourne. Its on-premises offering describes delivery of weights, serving infrastructure and applications, including isolated deployments. This is useful evidence of the proposed control boundary, but the pages reviewed do not establish an independently audited, source-by-source training history. Request exact artifacts and base/teacher declarations rather than equating “Australian-made” with a complete lineage audit. [Maincode company description](https://maincode.com/company), [Matilda on-premises offering](https://maincode.com/matilda-on-prem).

**Firmus / Project Southgate** represents a different role: compute and data-center development with CDC and NVIDIA. Its October 2025 announcement described planned expansion to **1.6 GW through 2028**. A March 2026 update disclosed a hyperscale customer contract for approximately 18,400 GB300 GPUs and continued Tasmania construction. These establish announced commercial commitments, not proof that the entire capacity roadmap is available. [Southgate expansion](https://firmus.co/newsroom/southgate-expansion), [March 2026 customer agreement](https://firmus.co/newsroom/firmus-signs-multi-year-agreement-with-global-hyperscale-customer-at-project-southgate).

**Sovsrc implication:** evaluate Maincode as a potential model/deployment partner and Firmus as a potential infrastructure supplier. A local publisher or research organization should anchor the content use case. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge requires community-led governance; national hosting alone is insufficient.

## France and Germany: commercial champions with regional ambitions

### France: Mistral

France's February 2025 AI summit announced **€109 billion** of infrastructure and deployment investments. This is an announced aggregate, not a single government appropriation, a Mistral training budget or evidence of completed capacity. [Élysée summit account](https://www.elysee.fr/emmanuel-macron/2025/02/11/faire-de-la-france-une-puissance-de-lia).

Mistral is a strong example of a private company performing a national and European strategic role. Its **11 August 2026** announcement combines regional inference, open-model access and multi-year commitments for European compute, with a target of up to **1 GW by 2030**. Regional endpoints are described as generally available, but processing arrangements permit specified safeguarded transfers to subprocessors outside the region. Its platform also announced support for Z.ai's GLM-5.2. Consequently, a French supplier or European endpoint does not establish French model lineage. [Mistral announcement](https://mistral.ai/news/regional-inference-open-models-new-compute/).

**Sovsrc implication:** Mistral is a commercial architecture and partnership comparator. Review each checkpoint and contract individually; an open-weight offering, proprietary service and third-party model route can have different permissions. European infrastructure is not evidence that source contributors receive rewards.

### Germany: Aleph Alpha, Cohere and Schwarz Digits

On **16 September 2026**, Cohere and Aleph Alpha announced a definitive business combination agreement, with proposed headquarters in Toronto and Berlin and continued research in Heidelberg. The release says the transaction remains subject to final regulatory approvals; this review did not establish completion. It also describes a sovereign-cloud partnership with Schwarz Group's **STACKIT**. [Combination agreement announcement](https://cohere.com/blog/cohere-and-aleph-alpha-sign-agreement).

This is a significant governance pattern: countries can seek autonomy through an allied commercial platform rather than insist on a company confined to one jurisdiction. For procurement, examine actual control rights, contractual continuity and administrative access after any transaction. The agreement alone does not settle those questions for a particular customer.

## Arab states: several distinct approaches

### Saudi Arabia: HUMAIN, ALLAM and SDAIA

**HUMAIN is PIF-owned**, launched on **12 May 2025** with a mandate spanning data centers, infrastructure, cloud, models and applications. It is a state-owned commercial national project, not merely a private startup receiving a grant. [PIF launch announcement](https://www.pif.gov.sa/en/news-and-insights/press-releases/2025/hrh-crown-prince-launches-humain-as-global-ai-powerhouse/).

NVIDIA's initial partnership announcement projected up to **500 MW over five years**, beginning with an 18,000-GB300 deployment. It also described a separate SDAIA AI factory initiative. These are distinct projects and projected deployment figures, not interchangeable measurements of current HUMAIN capacity. [NVIDIA partnership announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/saudi-arabia-and-nvidia-to-build-ai-factories-to-power-next-wave-of-intelligence-for-the-age-of-reasoning).

HUMAIN's IQ platform offers ALLAM-based Arabic capabilities, APIs, document processing and customization; the provider says its models run inside Saudi Arabia. That establishes a marketed location/control proposition. It does not establish unrestricted model redistribution, teacher use or a contributor royalty entitlement. [HUMAIN IQ](https://www.humain.com/iq).

**Sovsrc implication:** a possible later-stage customer or distribution partner for a licensed Arabic knowledge collection. Require an exact deployment contract and an Arabic source-rights registry before model adaptation.

### UAE: public models, commercial models and foreign-model infrastructure

Three separate tracks matter:

- **TII / Falcon:** TII is ATRC's applied research institute. Its January 2026 Falcon H1R 7B release demonstrates a public research route to downloadable models. The specific model card identifies the **Falcon LLM License**; do not assume every Falcon checkpoint uses Apache 2.0. [TII release](https://www.tii.ae/news/tii-launches-falcon-reasoning-best-7b-ai-model-globally-also-outperforms-larger-models), [model card](https://huggingface.co/tiiuae/Falcon-H1R-7B).
- **Jais 2:** Inception, a G42 company, MBZUAI and Cerebras announced the 70-billion-parameter Arabic model in December 2025. They describe development from the ground up and training/serving on Cerebras systems. That is the developers' account, not a full audit of source or synthetic-teacher permissions. [MBZUAI release](https://mbzuai.ac.ae/news-events/news/inception-cerebras-mbzuai-release-jais-2-the-next-generation-the-worlds-leading).
- **G42 / Stargate UAE:** the announced 1-GW cluster sits within the larger 5-GW UAE–US campus. G42's announcement assigns construction to G42 and operation to OpenAI and Oracle, with the first 200 MW targeted for 2026. These figures are nested, not additive. The reviewed announcement does not confirm commissioning. [G42 project announcement](https://www.g42.ai/resources/news/global-tech-alliance-launches-stargate-uae).

G42 is a commercial strategic platform with international shareholders and partnerships. Microsoft's 2024 announcement of a **US$1.5 billion minority investment and board participation** demonstrates why it should not be described as a wholly government-owned equivalent of HUMAIN. [Microsoft investment announcement](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2024/04/15/microsoft-and-g42-partner-to-accelerate-ai-innovation-in-uae-and-beyond/).

**Sovsrc implication:** TII/MBZUAI are research comparators; Inception/G42 are possible commercial counterparts; Stargate is a foreign-model infrastructure route. They confer different rights and cannot be evaluated as one “UAE model.”

### Qatar: Qai infrastructure and Fanar cultural capability

**Qai**, a subsidiary of the Qatar Investment Authority, and Brookfield announced a **US$20 billion joint venture** on **9 December 2025** for AI infrastructure in Qatar and selected international markets. This is a state-owned commercial vehicle working with an international capital and operating partner. The announcement does not establish that US$20 billion has been spent or that the planned facilities are operating. [QIA announcement](https://www.qia.qa/en/Newsroom/Pages/Brookfield-and-Qai-Form-%2420-Billion-Strategic-Investment-Partnership-for-AI-Infrastructure.aspx).

**Fanar** is a separate QCRI/HBKU research and deployment initiative sponsored by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology. HBKU describes Arabic dialect and cultural capabilities, cloud and on-premises deployment, and an enhanced Fanar 2.0 platform. Its partner material includes Qatar National Library, Al Jazeera and other knowledge institutions. Institutional collaboration is relevant to a content consortium, but is not evidence of individual author consent or revenue sharing. [Fanar overview](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/en/web-summit-2026/technologies/fanar), [Fanar 2.0](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/en/news/hbku-releases-enhanced-flagship-generative-ai-platform), [institutional partner overview](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/fanar.pdf).

**Sovsrc implication:** Fanar is a particularly relevant governance comparator for a language-focused knowledge syndicate. Qai is an infrastructure counterpart, not interchangeable with the research institution or content owners.

### Egypt: Karnak shows why national branding is not a lineage audit

Egypt's ministry-affiliated Applied Innovation Center announced the **Karnak Chat pilot on 23 August 2026**. It explicitly describes enhancing existing global open models and possible operation within local data centers. [AIC pilot announcement](https://www.aic.gov.eg/news/43/).

The reviewed **Karnak-40B-v1.0** model card identifies **Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507** as its base and reports depth extension, continued pretraining and instruction tuning. This is direct evidence of foreign learned-weight ancestry despite its Egyptian public mission. [Developer model card](https://huggingface.co/Applied-Innovation-Center/Karnak-40B-v1.0).

**Sovsrc implication:** useful as a localization comparator; ineligible under the project's current no-Chinese-model-lineage condition. This finding concerns the named checkpoint, not every future Egyptian model.

## Other instructive national models

### Switzerland: the strongest public-transparency comparator in this review

**ETH Zurich, EPFL and CSCS** released Apertus in September 2025 with published development resources and a stated process for respecting machine-readable opt-outs and filtering personal data. Public availability and an opt-out process should not be equated with every creator's affirmative permission to train. [Original release and data approach](https://ethz.ch/en/news-and-events/eth-news/news/2025/09/press-release-apertus-a-fully-open-transparent-multilingual-language-model.html).

ETH reports **Apertus 1.5 released on 24 July 2026**, trained on CSCS's Alps system, with Apache 2.0 licensing and development transparency. It also identifies public-administration and newsroom uses. This provides a useful public-interest pattern: shared research infrastructure, inspectable models and practical local services. The openness assessment here follows the institutions' published account; each data artifact and model variant still needs inspection. [Apertus 1.5 release](https://ai.ethz.ch/news-and-events/ai-center-news/2026/07/apertus-15-building-the-next-generation-of-open-ai-infrastructure.html).

**Assessment:** learn from the documentation and public-infrastructure model. Creator rewards would require an additional participation agreement and accounting system. Apertus is not US-origin and is not automatically admitted to sovsrc's model pool.

### UK: invest in a portfolio and shared compute

The UK announced initial support through its **£500 million Sovereign AI Unit** in April 2026, combining backing for domestic companies with access to research compute. The AI Research Resource includes Isambard-AI and other facilities. This is a national capability portfolio, rather than evidence that one company owns a national LLM on the government's behalf. [Government announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ai-firms-pioneering-drug-discovery-cheaper-supercomputing-and-more-get-first-backing-through-uks-sovereign-ai), [AIRR infrastructure program](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/expression-of-interest-airr-heterogeneous-supercomputer-host-site-selection/expression-of-interest-airr-heterogeneous-supercomputer-host-site-selection).

**Lesson:** sovereignty can support specialized scientific and enterprise capabilities instead of funding only a general-purpose chatbot.

### India: national funding, commercial developers and academic consortia

India's **IndiaAI Mission** has an approved outlay of **₹10,372 crore**. A February 2026 government account lists twelve selected foundation-model organizations, including Sarvam and the IIT Bombay-led BharatGen consortium. This is a diversified public–private approach to Indian-language and domestic model capability. [Government program account](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2227612&lang=2&reg=3).

Sarvam is also a commercial national-champion example: its June 2026 financing announcement describes a business spanning training infrastructure, model research and enterprise/government distribution. It reported a **US$234 million first close toward a US$300 million Series B**, which should not be restated as a fully closed US$300 million round. [Sarvam announcement](https://www.sarvam.ai/announcing-series-b).

**Lesson:** combine language expertise, committed compute and an application market. Government selection is not proof that every training input or output use is commercially unrestricted.

### Singapore: regional language capability as a public program

IMDA's **National Multimodal LLM Programme**, with AI Singapore and A*STAR, supports SEA-LION and MERaLiON. The aim includes Southeast Asian languages, cultural context, research capability and talent. [IMDA program](https://www.imda.gov.sg/about-imda/emerging-technologies-and-research/national-multimodal-llm-programme).

**Lesson:** a country can build valuable regional capability without treating English-language frontier rankings as the only objective. Inspect the exact family member's base model and license before selecting a training foundation; a family name is not a provenance declaration.

### Japan: SoftBank as a commercial builder of national capability

SoftBank's account of **SB Intuitions / Sarashina** describes domestic Japanese-language foundation-model development and a multi-model strategy. Its 2025 report discusses a 460-billion-parameter development effort and a smaller commercial path. These are company-reported development claims, not an independent quality ranking. [SoftBank report](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/documents/integrated_reports/fy2025/tamba/).

**Lesson:** telecom distribution, compute investment and local language research can reinforce each other. Domestic model development must be distinguished from the same corporate group's work distributing another company's models.

### South Korea: sovereignty as an explicit qualification requirement

The **MSIT Sovereign AI Foundation Model project** supports commercial consortia with compute and data. Its first evaluation explained why adapting foreign weights alone did not meet the program's definition, and required attention to originality, licensing and disclosure. LG AI Research, SK Telecom and Upstage advanced at that stage; Naver did not satisfy the stated sovereignty requirements. [Official first-phase decision](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?bbsSeqNo=42&mId=4&mPid=2&nttSeqNo=1212&sCode=eng).

The subsequent second-phase disclosure covers **Motif, Upstage, SK Telecom and LG**, using benchmarks plus expert and user evaluation. The cohort and evaluation stage should always accompany any ranking. [Second-phase score disclosure](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?bbsSeqNo=42&mId=4&mPid=2&nttSeqNo=1301&sCode=eng).

**Lesson:** make independence an evidence gate. Sovsrc should similarly review weights, teachers and data before calling a model independently sourced, while distinguishing use of research methods from inheritance of learned parameters.

## US companies as exporters of national AI infrastructure

**OpenAI for Countries** explicitly offers to help governments develop sovereign AI capability in coordination with the US government. The UAE was its first announced partnership. This makes OpenAI a commercial actor with a national strategic and diplomatic role; it does not make OpenAI a US government-owned laboratory or transfer its foundation-model rights to partner countries. [OpenAI's UAE announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-stargate-uae/).

**NVIDIA** supplies much of the enabling compute and software in the examples above. Its role is closer to a common industrial supplier across national programs than a single country's LLM project. **Palantir** supplies deployment and organizational governance capabilities; the [existing Palantir assessment](PALANTIR_SOVEREIGN_AI_RESEARCH.md) examines the contractual and architectural boundaries in detail.

This creates two different negotiating positions: a supplier wants a durable international platform market, while the purchasing country wants continuity, control and domestic value. Sovsrc should retain its source registry, agreements, evidence and ledger independently of whichever platform serves the model. Model-training permission must be obtained through the applicable license or executed contract; a “sovereign” service description is insufficient.

## What is missing for IP, ideas and content rewards?

Across the reviewed material, the best-documented controls concern compute, data location, model access, local languages and institutional governance. Evidence for a general **source-to-payment system** is much thinner. Content partnerships, open model releases and national IP-retention policies solve different problems from paying a particular author for qualifying use.

For this project, four evidence classes should remain separate:

| Event | Evidence that can support it | Appropriate contractual treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A source was included in training | Permission record, dataset version and actual consumed-training manifest | Agreed corpus participation credit or reward; no claim of exact causal influence on each answer |
| A source supported a delivered answer | Authorized retrieval, source version, relevant passage and validated receipt | Eligible-use payment under the source agreement |
| An idea or method influenced later work | Human-reviewed adoption/reuse record, participant statements and dispute process | Agreed contribution or influence share |
| A country hosts or subsidizes AI | Facility and funding evidence | Infrastructure or public-benefit accounting; not a substitute for individual source rights |

Payments for ideas and influences would arise from accepted participation agreements. The SIP proposal does not itself create a new statutory property right. Likewise, a library, ministry or publisher must have the relevant authority for the material it contributes; institutional custody alone is not enough.

Indigenous sovereignty also deserves its own authority model. The **CARE Principles** emphasize collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility and ethics. For Canada and Australia especially, design participation with the affected communities; do not assume national institutions can authorize community knowledge or that financial reward substitutes for consent. [Global Indigenous Data Alliance](https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples).

## Recommended pathway for Sovereign Source

**Keep the first product focused on an auditable content service that can connect to national infrastructure.** The [startup plan](STARTUP_GO_TO_MARKET_PLAN.md) starts with specialist publishers; the evidence here supports treating national projects as later research, infrastructure and distribution counterparts rather than assuming immediate government demand.

| Priority | Counterpart to investigate | Concrete first discussion | Evidence needed before commitment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research design | Swiss AI Initiative; QCRI/HBKU; TII/MBZUAI | Source-manifest and attribution evaluation on a small authorized corpus | Available artifacts, source authority, publication and experiment permissions |
| Enterprise integration | Cohere; Mistral; Maincode | Connect a permissions API and return source receipts with answers | External-customer rights, retention, model route, deployment control and exit terms |
| Regional content consortium | Publishers, universities and libraries in Canada, Australia, France or an Arabic-language market | Licensed retrieval pilot with contributor statements | Authority to license each use, beneficiary identity, community governance and agreed splits |
| Infrastructure procurement | Firmus/CDC; Qai; HUMAIN; G42; relevant public compute programs | Capacity and operating-boundary assessment after workload sizing | Actual availability, allocation eligibility, operator access, cost and portability |

These are investigation priorities, not claims of existing partnerships or willingness to participate.

Use the existing [production architecture](PRODUCTION_SYSTEM_PLAN.md): Python/Pydantic for typed source and permission records, Pydantic AI for approved inference, Prefect for ingestion and review workflows, Rust/Axum for authoritative policy APIs, and PostgreSQL for accepted attribution and financial records. A national deployment should add explicit fields for jurisdiction, hosting/operator identity, community authority, language, permission scope, model revision and expiry.

A bounded pilot should demonstrate one complete path: a contributor enrolls an authorized work; a permitted answer uses it; the system issues a source receipt; the accepted reward rule creates a ledger entry; the contributor can inspect and challenge the statement. Training should follow only after source permissions and actual consumption records can be proved. Unknown pretraining ancestry cannot be repaired merely by adding citations at inference time.

The broader geographic review **does not change sovsrc's existing US-origin and no-Chinese-learned-lineage admission conditions**. International programs are comparators and potential commercial/research counterparts here. Karnak's disclosed Qwen base fails the lineage condition; a Mistral-hosted third-party route must be checked independently. No additional base model or teacher is approved by this document. The [model selection summary](MODEL_SELECTION_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md) remains the decision record.

## Evidence gaps to resolve before procurement

Obtain current commissioning records for advertised capacity; confirm the Cohere–Aleph Alpha transaction status; request exact checkpoint and teacher manifests; inspect source permissions and commercial licenses; and ask whether any proposed partner can demonstrate contributor-level settlements. Public announcements generally cannot answer all of these questions.

This research establishes a comparative landscape and a practical role for SIP. It does not establish that any evaluated program meets the project's complete provenance, independence or creator-rewards requirements.
